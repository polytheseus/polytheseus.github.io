Making Tax Digital for landlords: are you over £30,000?

From 6 April 2027, landlords with more than £30,000 of rent and self-employment income have to keep digital records and send HMRC an update every three months. The number that decides it is in the tax return you're about to file. Here's how to work it out, with a free calculator.

The short answer. Add up the rent you received from 6 April 2025 to 5 April 2026 (your share, before any expenses or agent fees) and any self-employment turnover. If the total is more than £30,000, you need Making Tax Digital for Income Tax from 6 April 2027. If it's £20,000 or less, you're exempt. In between, next year's figure decides April 2028.

HMRC checks the 2025 to 2026 return you file by 31 January 2027 and writes to you. If no letter comes, it's still your job to sign up (gov.uk).

What counts and what doesn't

Counts towards the £30,000Doesn't count
  • Rent from UK property, before expenses, mortgage interest and agent fees
  • Your share only of jointly owned property
  • Rent from property abroad, if you're UK resident
  • Self-employment turnover from any sole-trader work, even a small side business
  • Income from a property you sold during the year, if you still have another property or self-employment
  • Lodger income over the Rent a Room limit, or that you put on your return
  • Wages, salary and anything taxed through PAYE
  • State and private pensions
  • Dividends, including from your own company
  • Your profit share from a partnership
  • Rent received by a limited company (companies aren't in MTD for Income Tax)
  • Income from REITs and property funds
  • Lodger income under £7,500 (£3,750 if shared) that you didn't declare

Sources: Work out your qualifying income (HMRC, updated 11 September 2026), Rent a Room Scheme, Create digital records.

Married couples and civil partners: rent from a jointly owned property is normally split 50:50 for tax. If you've told HMRC on Form 17 that you own it in different shares, use your actual share.

If you're in from April 2027: what happens when

WhenWhat
By 31 January 2027File your 2025 to 2026 return the usual way. This is the one HMRC uses to decide.
Early 2027HMRC writes if you're over £30,000. Sign up through gov.uk (or your agent does) and choose software.
6 April 2027Start digital records of rent and expenses (1 April if your accounts run to 31 March).
7 August 2027Quarterly update 1 (6 April to 5 July 2027)
7 November 2027Update 2 (the year so far, to 5 October)
31 January 2028Your 2026 to 2027 return, filed the usual way. The last one outside MTD.
7 February 2028Update 3 (the year so far, to 5 January)
7 May 2028Update 4 (the whole year, to 5 April 2028)
31 January 20292027 to 2028 tax return, through your MTD software. Tax is still paid on 31 January and 31 July.

Quarterly updates are totals worked out by your software, not tax returns. You send one even if nothing happened that quarter. All your UK properties count as one property business, so you send one update for them all, not one per property. Jointly let property: you can send just the income each quarter and add the expenses before filing the return (send quarterly updates, HMRC, updated 16 October 2026).

Penalties: Budget 2026 (28 October 2026) said that if you join in April 2027, late quarterly updates for 2027 to 2028 won't earn penalty points. You still have to send all four before you can file that year's return (Budget 2026 OOTLAR, measure 2.36). After that first year, each missed quarterly deadline earns a point, and 4 points means a £200 penalty, then £200 for each further miss. Late payment penalties start at 15 days late, but in your first year you get 30 days to pay or set up a payment plan (MTD penalties, HMRC).

The records you'll need to keep

A digital record for each rent payment and each expense: the date, the amount and the category. Below £90,000 of turnover you can record just "income" or "expense", but residential landlords also have to flag finance costs (mortgage interest), because those get a tax reduction (20% in 2026 to 2027) instead of being deducted.

Rental profit will be taxed at higher rates from April 2027. Budget 2025 created separate property rates of Income Tax, and Budget 2026 set them for 2027 to 2028 at 24% basic, 44% higher and 49% additional in England, Wales and Northern Ireland (2026 to 2027: the normal 20%, 40% and 45%). So a basic-rate landlord with £10,000 of taxable rental profit would pay about £2,400 instead of £2,000. Scotland and Wales can set their own property rates (Budget 2026 OOTLAR, measure 1.2, 28 October 2026).

  • Income: rent, plus anything else tenants pay you for the let, such as premiums.
  • Expenses (the same categories as the property pages of the tax return): rent, rates, insurance and ground rent; repairs and maintenance; finance costs; legal, management and professional fees (agent fees go here); services such as cleaning and utilities you pay; travel; other.
  • Keep the bank statements and invoices as well, as you do now.

Spreadsheets are allowed with "bridging" software that sends the figures from the sheet to HMRC. The figures must move digitally: linked cells, CSV import or an upload are fine; retyping or copy-and-paste between sheets is not. Find options with HMRC's software finder. HMRC doesn't recommend products, and neither do we. If you'd like a sheet that's already laid out in HMRC's categories, ours is here (£14).

Common mistakes

  • Using profit. "I only make £12,000 after the mortgage" doesn't matter. The test is rent before costs.
  • Using what the agent pays you. Use the rent the tenant paid. Add the agent's fee back.
  • Counting the whole of a joint property. Only your share counts.
  • Forgetting the side business. £18,000 of rent and £13,000 of freelance work is £31,000: in.
  • Counting the deposit. A deposit you hold for the tenant isn't rent. Any part you keep for damage or unpaid rent usually is income: ask your accountant how to treat it.
  • Waiting for the letter. HMRC says it's your responsibility to check and sign up even if you don't get one.

FAQ

Do landlords need Making Tax Digital from April 2027?

Yes, if your qualifying income for 2025 to 2026 is more than £30,000. That's your gross rent (your share) plus any self-employment turnover, from the return due on 31 January 2027.

Is the threshold based on rent or profit?

Rent. £32,000 of rent with £14,000 of costs is still over £30,000.

What if I own property through a limited company?

MTD for Income Tax is for individuals who file Self Assessment. Rent your company receives is in its Corporation Tax return, not your qualifying income. Dividends the company pays you don't count either.

I sold my rental during the year. Does it still count?

If you still have other property or self-employment income, yes: gov.uk says income from a source that stopped is included. If all your property and self-employment income stopped, tell HMRC before it signs you up.

Can I be exempt if I'm not good with computers?

Only if it isn't reasonable for you to use software at all, for example because of age, disability or no internet access where you live. HMRC won't accept "I've always filed on paper" or "it costs time and money". You apply for this digital exclusion exemption, and someone can help you, such as an agent, friend or family member.

Does MTD apply in Scotland, Wales and Northern Ireland?

Yes. MTD for Income Tax is a UK-wide HMRC rule, so the thresholds are the same everywhere.
