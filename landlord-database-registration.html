The landlord database: when to register, what it costs, what to have ready

From 15 December 2026, private landlords in England have to register themselves and every let property on the government's new Register your rental property service (often called the PRS Database or landlord register). It opens one region at a time. This free planner tells you your window, works out your first fee with the service's own formula, and writes a checklist for each property.

The short answer. Each region gets a 3-month window, starting with the West Midlands on 15 December 2026 and ending with the South West on 14 November 2027. Register yourself (free) and each let property (£65 a year per property, no VAT) before your region's window closes. During the roll-out the first fee is reduced, because no days before 15 November 2027 are charged: registering on 15 December 2026 costs £5.34.

You'll need a GOV.UK One Login and, for each property, its gas safety record, electrical safety report and EPC. Missing details can be added within 28 days. Source: gov.uk, Register your rental property service: guidance for landlords (29 October 2026).

How the fee works

  • £65 per property per year, not subject to VAT. Registering yourself as a landlord and updating your records are free.
  • One renewal date for everything: a year from the day you register your first property. A second property added two months later renews on the same date, and its first fee only covers the days up to it.
  • The roll-out discount: the service doesn't charge for any days before 15 November 2027, the date by which every landlord must be registered. Its formula is (£65 ÷ days in the year) × (days to your renewal date − days before 15 November 2027), rounded down to the penny.
  • gov.uk's examples: register on 15 December 2026 and you pay £5.34 (30 charged days of 365); register on 25 June 2027 and you pay £39.60 (223 of 366). The planner gives the same answers.
  • Early doesn't save money over time. Whenever you register, you pay for the days from 15 November 2027 onwards. Registering early just means a smaller first bill and an earlier renewal date. The real cost is missing your window.
  • It's tax-deductible: gov.uk says to check HMRC's guidance when you do your tax return. Keep the receipt with your property expenses.

Who has to register

  • Private landlords renting a property in England to tenants on an assured or regulated tenancy: individuals, sole traders, partnerships, and organisations (companies, charities, trusts, LLPs).
  • Joint landlords each register. One adds the property and invites the others. If you own part of a let property and are entitled to possession, you register as a joint landlord even if you're not named on the tenancy agreement.
  • HMOs: one registration for the whole property, not one per room.
  • Letting agents can't do it for you. You start the registration and pay; once you have the registration number and the home is let, your agent can add the tenancy and safety information. You're still responsible for the record being complete within 28 days.
  • Empty homes: during the roll-out (15 December 2026 to 14 November 2027) you only have to register properties that are let or become let. Later, empty homes will have to be registered before they're advertised, and adverts will need the landlord and property numbers. gov.uk will update its guidance before those rules start.
  • Not covered: supported exempt accommodation (Section 12 of the Supported Housing (Regulatory Oversight) Act 2023). Lodgers in your own home aren't assured tenants.

After you register: the 28-day rules

  • Anything you skipped at registration must be added within 28 days, or the property registration may be deactivated. If the home is let, that puts you in breach. Your landlord registration is deactivated too if you have no active properties.
  • Any change or expiry must be updated within 28 days: a new tenancy, a rent change, a renewed gas certificate, a new EICR, a new agent, your own new address. Updates are free.
  • A simple habit: whenever a certificate is renewed or the rent changes, update the database the same week.

What happens if you don't

Not registering yourself or a property can mean a penalty of up to £7,000. Knowingly or recklessly giving false or misleading information, or continuing or repeated breaches, can mean up to £40,000, and your council can take enforcement action. Once a region's window opens, tenants there can also apply for a rent repayment order for those false-information and repeated-breach cases. Landlords with a live banning order may not be allowed to register.

For now your details aren't shown publicly, but gov.uk says some information about you and your property may be viewable in future.

What this doesn't cover

  • Wales has Rent Smart Wales, Scotland has the Scottish landlord register and Northern Ireland has the landlord registration scheme, each with its own fees and rules.
  • Social housing and supported exempt accommodation.
  • Council licensing (HMO, additional and selective licences) is separate and still applies: the database just records your licence.

FAQ

When do I have to register on the landlord database?

By the end of your property's regional window: 14 March 2027 for the West Midlands, then a month later for each region in turn, ending with the South West on 14 November 2027. See the table above.

How much does it cost to register a rental property?

£65 per property per year, with no VAT. Your landlord registration is free. During the roll-out the first fee is reduced because days before 15 November 2027 aren't charged.

Do I need to register an empty property?

Not during the roll-out, unless it becomes let. Later, empty properties will have to be registered before they're advertised.

Can my letting agent register for me?

No. You start each property registration and pay. Your agent can then add tenancy and safety information, but you're responsible for the record being complete within 28 days.

We own the property jointly. Do we both register?

Yes, each joint landlord registers. One of you registers the property and invites the others.

What happens if I miss my window?

Not registering can mean a penalty of up to £7,000 from your council. If you've missed it, register as soon as you can rather than waiting.
