Landlord tax from April 2027: what the new property rates cost you

Budget 2026 confirmed that rental profit gets its own, higher Income Tax rates from 6 April 2027. Here's what changes, who it hits hardest, and a free calculator that puts your own figures through both years.

The short answer. From 6 April 2027, rental profit in England, Wales and Northern Ireland is taxed at 24%, 44% and 49% instead of 20%, 40% and 45%. The mortgage interest credit rises from 20% to 24%. So the extra tax is roughly 4p for every £1 of taxed rental profit, minus 4p for every £1 of mortgage interest you get the credit on.

A landlord with £10,000 of profit and no mortgage pays about £400 a year more. With a large mortgage, much less. The rates were announced at Budget 2026 and become law when Finance Bill 2026-27 passes (gov.uk).

Three things change, not one

  1. Higher rates on rental profit. 4 percentage points more in every band.
  2. A bigger mortgage interest credit. You still can't deduct mortgage interest on a home you let; you get a credit instead, now 24%. It's capped at the lowest of your finance costs, your rental profit and your income over the Personal Allowance, and anything unused carries forward.
  3. Your Personal Allowance goes on other income first. From 2027 to 2028 the £12,570 is set against wages, pensions and self-employment before rental profit. That matters most to landlords with a small pension or part-time job: the part of their income that gets taxed is now rental profit at 24%, not other income at 20%.

Tom's case is the one that stings. His cash left after costs and interest is £7,800, and tax takes £4,560 of it now (58%) and £4,872 from 2027 (62%), because 44% is charged on his profit before interest but the interest only gets a 24% credit. Each example is in the calculator's tests.

Who's hit hardest

  • Landlords without a mortgage pay the full 4p per £1 of profit: nothing comes back through the credit.
  • Higher-rate landlords with big mortgages pay less extra in pounds, but the gap between 44% on profit and a 24% credit means tax takes a bigger share of the cash they actually keep. Some already pay tax while losing money in cash terms.
  • Landlords with small pensions lose the benefit of their unused allowance being set against rent at the lower rate.
  • Landlords between £100,000 and £125,140 are in the Personal Allowance taper. Each extra £1 of rental profit costs 60p in tax now and 64p from 2027: 44p on the profit itself, plus 20p because 50p of allowance is lost and your other income (taxed at 40%) is what that allowance was covering.

What you can do before April 2027

None of this is advice: these are the questions to take to an accountant.

  • Claim every allowable cost. Each £1 of genuine running cost saves 24p or 44p from 2027, not 20p or 40p. Agent fees, repairs, insurance, safety certificates, ground rent, service charges, accountancy, travel to the property all count. Improvements don't.
  • Keep records you can trust. Landlords with more than £30,000 of rent need Making Tax Digital from April 2027 anyway (check with our free calculator), so quarterly records are coming.
  • Joint ownership. Rent from property owned with a spouse or civil partner is split 50:50 for tax unless you own it in unequal shares and tell HMRC (Form 17). If one of you pays a lower rate, that split matters more now.
  • Raising the rent. The calculator shows the rise that would cover the extra tax. In England, increases for periodic tenancies go through Section 13 and Form 4A, and the tenant can challenge them at tribunal.
  • Incorporation and selling up are big decisions with Stamp Duty Land Tax, Capital Gains Tax and mortgage costs of their own. Don't make them from a headline rate.

FAQ

How much is tax on rental income going up in April 2027?

From 6 April 2027 rental profit has its own rates in England, Wales and Northern Ireland: 24% basic, 44% higher and 49% additional, up from 20%, 40% and 45%. That's 4p more on every pound of taxed rental profit. The mortgage interest credit rises from 20% to 24% at the same time, which gives some of it back.

What are the property income tax rates for 2027 to 2028?

Property basic rate 24%, property higher rate 44%, property additional rate 49%, announced at Budget 2026 on 28 October 2026 and due to be legislated in Finance Bill 2026-27. The bands don't change: basic rate on the first £37,700 of taxable income, additional rate over £125,140, Personal Allowance £12,570.

Does the mortgage interest tax credit change in 2027?

Yes. Landlords can't deduct residential mortgage interest; they get a tax credit at the basic rate instead. From 2027 to 2028 it's given at the property basic rate, so 24% instead of 20%. It's still capped at the lowest of your finance costs, your rental profit and your income above the Personal Allowance.

Is my Personal Allowance used differently from April 2027?

Yes. From 2027 to 2028 allowances and reliefs are set against wages, pensions and self-employment first, and only what's left covers rental, savings and dividend income. If your other income is under £12,570, the part of your income that gets taxed is now rental profit at 24%, not other income at 20%.

Do the new property rates apply in Scotland and Wales?

The 24/44/49% rates are set for England, Wales and Northern Ireland. Scotland and Wales have been given the power to set their own property rates; Scottish taxpayers currently pay Scottish rates on rental profit, and the Scottish Parliament hasn't announced 2027 to 2028 property rates yet.

Does this affect landlords with a limited company?

No. Rent received by a company is taxed through Corporation Tax, which these changes don't touch. Dividends you take from the company are taxed at the dividend rates, which rose in April 2026. Moving existing property into a company usually means Stamp Duty Land Tax and Capital Gains Tax, so get advice first.
